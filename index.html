MixtapeMeetups

A structured, peer-led, song-based recovery format. Nobody has built one. We are.

The premise

From optimization to feeling.

A world of noise. Dopamine loops. Too much coming at everyone at once. People cannot settle, and another escape does not fix it. What fixes it is a practice you come back to, again and again.

Recovery is the only field built entirely around coming back. Same room, same people, every week, for years.

The practice already exists. Nobody has put music at the center of it.
01 · Mechanism

Addiction acts on the reward pathway. So does music.

Same wiring

Music changes activity in the brain's reward system. The same system a drug acts on.

The blocker

Music runs on the body's own opioid system. Block those receptors with naltrexone and people rate their own favorite songs as less pleasurable. Their neutral music barely changes.

Their song

Scans caught dopamine releasing at the exact moment a song gave someone chills. Their own favorite song, not one picked for them.

Block the opioid system and the pleasure measurably drops. That is not a metaphor. That is pharmacology.
The dopamine loop is the problem everyone names. It is also the lever.
Mallik, Chanda and Levitin, Scientific Reports, 2017 · Salimpoor et al., Nature Neuroscience, 2011

04 · The hour

What a peer-led music hour does.

  • Everyone listens

    The song plays at the top of the meeting. Same song, same room, same hour every week.

  • A memory arrives

    It gets there before the defense does.

  • The producer goes first

    They answer the prompt honestly before anyone else does. That is what gives the room permission.

  • Someone speaks

    Nobody is asked to open up. They talk about the song, and it comes out anyway.

  • They come back

    Next week there is another song.

A clinical group asks how you are doing, and people arm themselves before answering. A song asks nothing.
Listen ahead of time if you want and walk in with something already on your mind. Deep listening, then guided reflection, every week in the same room.
05 · The language

Four words run the whole thing.

Side A

The listen. Together at the top of the meeting, or ahead of time on your own.

Side B

The discussion. The producer answers the prompt first, and answers it honestly.

Liner notes

The workbook. Prompts for every song, with room to write.

The producer

A trained peer who runs the session and goes first. Shapes a record, never performs.

Anyone can cite the research. Nobody else owns the method, the words or the training. A format nobody can name is a format nobody can buy twice.
